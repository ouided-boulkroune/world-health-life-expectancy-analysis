# 🌍 World Health & Life Expectancy Analysis (2000-2015)

## 📋 Project Overview
This project analyzes global health indicators and life expectancy data 
from 193 countries between 2000 and 2015, using data collected by the 
World Health Organization (WHO).

The goal is to understand what factors affect how long people live — 
including GDP, education, vaccination rates, and country development status.

---

## 🔍 Key Questions Answered
- How did global life expectancy change from 2000 to 2015?
- What is the difference between developed and developing countries?
- Does GDP affect how long people live?
- Does education level affect life expectancy?
- Which countries have the highest and lowest life expectancy?

---

## 💡 Key Findings
- Global life expectancy improved from **66.8 to 71.6 years** between 2000 and 2015
- Developed countries live on average **12 years longer** than developing countries
- **Education is the strongest factor** — more schooling = longer life
- GDP matters most at low income levels — after a certain point extra wealth adds little
- **Sierra Leone** has the lowest life expectancy (46 years) vs **Japan/Sweden** at 83 years
